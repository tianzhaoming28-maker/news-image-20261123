## How the eight were chosen

The lead is the day's most consequential new development: Ukraine
taking the war 2,400 km from its front line to a Russian warship in
a Libyan port, announced by Zelenskyy on Monday. The rest of the
page balances regions and beats — a disaster in Southeast Asia, a
major aid pledge in South Asia, a US sanctions move in the Middle
East, the climate talks' aftermath in the Pacific, a cultural
reopening in Japan, a court ruling in Thailand, and a domestic
policy plan in China.

Date discipline mattered. Several big stories circulating on Monday
belong to Sunday and were left out: the drone strikes on Khartoum,
South Korea's education minister resigning over AI exam cheating,
the Netanyahu–Eisenkot coalition talks ending without a deal, and
the thousands marching in Istanbul against the media crackdown all
happened on Sunday 22 November. The COP31 collapse itself was also
Sunday's news; what belongs to Monday is the aftermath — the blame
in Australia and the despair in the Pacific — and that is what the
page shows.

## 1. Ukraine sinks Russian warship off Libya (LEAD)

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that Ukrainian forces
had sunk the Russian navy corvette Merkury at the Benghazi naval base
in eastern Libya, using seven sea drones launched from a "mother
ship" in the Mediterranean. The attack on Sunday also hit the dry
cargo ship MV Angara, which Kyiv says Moscow used to transport
weapons, and destroyed a Ka-27 combat helicopter. Libya's
eastern-based government condemned the attack; at least 10 people
were wounded.

## 3. India pledges $1.62bn to flood-hit Nepal

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced on Monday a $1.62bn
(240 billion Nepalese rupees) economic package for Nepal, after
talks in New Delhi with Nepali Prime Minister Balendra Shah — one
of India's largest-ever relief commitments, following August's
devastating floods that killed some 1,500 people and left 5,200
missing. The package includes a $377m grant and a $1.24bn line of
credit for energy projects.

## 4. US sanctions Iran's "shadow banking" networks

The US Treasury announced on Monday sanctions on more than two
dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and individuals
under Operation Economic Outcast, in a sweeping effort to disrupt
Iran's global shadow-banking networks. Treasury said the network,
overseen by the Central Bank of Iran, moves billions of dollars
for Tehran's foreign trade; its FinCEN analysis put potentially
linked activity at about $14.7bn from January 2025 to August 2026.
